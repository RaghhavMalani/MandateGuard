THE PROBLEM

An AI agent can decide what to buy.
Deciding is not the same as being allowed to pay.

Give an agent a budget and it will find you a product — and, sooner or later, a subscription you said you didn’t want, at a price that moved after you agreed to it, from a seller nobody can name. MandateGuard sits between the AI buyer and payment execution, and nothing the agent decides reaches a payment provider until MandateGuard has independently checked it against what you actually asked for.

How it works, in one purchase

  1. USER

    “Buy wireless headphones below ₹4,000.
    One-time payment only.”

  2. AI AGENT

    Searches the catalog. Finds a product. Proposes it.

    It has no payment credential and cannot charge anything.

  3. MANDATEGUARD

    Compares five things that have to agree:

    • 1 what you asked for
    • 2 what the agent proposed
    • 3 what the catalog says
    • 4 what the merchant can prove
    • 5 whether you still consent

    Then returns one of three answers.

ALLOW

The purchase is authorized and may proceed.

Everything agreed. A single-use, transaction-bound capability is issued, and only that capability can reach Razorpay.

BLOCK

The proposal violates your mandate.

Something the agent proposed contradicts something you said. No capability is issued and the payment provider is never called.

REVIEW

There is not enough trusted evidence to decide safely.

Nobody authoritative has said whether this charge repeats, or who is selling it. MandateGuard refuses to guess rather than guessing in your favour.
